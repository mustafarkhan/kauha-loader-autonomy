KAUHA · digital twin · simulated 60-minute shift

Autonomous wheel loader, loading trucks

A 15.5 t loader with a 3 m³ bucket loads 20 t trucks from two stockpiles. The full stack is in the loop: autonomy computer, CAN bus with end-to-end checks, drive-by-wire gateway, safety PLC with ray-cast safety scanners, and a remote supervisor. The scenario adds people, a radio drop, an unmapped obstacle, dust, a GNSS outage, a material change, a computer freeze and a remote e-stop.

Shift results

The same 60-minute scenario was run in five configurations, each on several random seeds: learned bucket controller; tuned rule-based controller; learned under winter braking (lower speed cap); learned with the field-aware speed guard on; and a stress case where people on foot come within 0.3 m of a stopped machine and 1 m of a moving one, instead of the default 1 m and 1.5 m. One shift is one sample: small timing differences change where a person crosses or which stop happens, so every number is a mean with its range. These are simulation outputs, not field data.

Reading these results

What the twin demonstrates

  • The safety channel stops the machine before people, independent of the autonomy computer. This was checked closed-loop at every speed band, forward and reverse, dry and winter.
  • Faults degrade to a safe state and come back: radio loss, a frozen autonomy computer, an unmapped obstacle and a GNSS outage.
  • The full CAN path, with end-to-end checks and timeouts, carries every command; killing the autonomy, remote or safety-PLC process in the multi-process test stops the machine.
  • Bucket filling: once the training objective used the cycle time the twin measured, a learned policy no longer beat a well-tuned rule set: it ties overall and is 7 % worse on crushed rock. It stays in the campaign so the comparison is visible in every run.
  • The twin finds safety-design errors before metal is cut. In this round it found that the side fields did not cover the rear frame, that teleop recovery could slide the bucket past a person standing beside the frame, and that the fix for that could lock the machine against a pile flank. Each is fixed and has a test.

What it does not prove

  • Productivity on a real machine. The soil model is a reduced FEE-style model, not validated against measured bucket forces.
  • Real braking and scanner response. These are assumptions until measured, dry and on snow.
  • Detection of real people in dust and snow. The scanners are ray-cast against ideal shapes.
  • How real people behave around the machine. Pedestrians follow scripted routes and a simple keep-clear rule; the stress case narrows that rule but is still a model.
  • Certification. The safety logic is a reference model to re-implement in a certified PLC toolchain.
