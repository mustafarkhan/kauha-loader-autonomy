## Notes

- **V-STD-01** (pass): margin 5 %. The winter planning value (1.50 m/s2) is the available friction on a slippery surface, not the brake's capacity, so it is not compared here. The brake itself has to be measured on the machine.
- **V-STD-02** (open): Confirmed from the 1985 edition's sample: the test speed is 32 km/h or the top speed if lower, and the distance is measured from the point where the brake control is actuated. The limit table is not legible there and later editions are not free. The commonly quoted later form (v2/150 + 0.2(v + 5), v in km/h) gives 7.6 m, which the assumed braking would meet measured the standard's way; it corresponds to a mean deceleration of about 2.3 m/s2, close to the 2.5 m/s2 the stop budget assumes. To close: read the limit in the purchased standard and measure the machine's brake.
- **V-STD-03** (pass): The fields are sized for the machine's stopping distance, as ISO 3691-4 does; a person walking into a machine that has already stopped is outside the stop function.
- **V-STD-04** (pass): 2D twin; a return counts only inside a sensor's rated coverage. The front and rear fields end up to 8.1 m from the scanners, beyond their 4 m rated range, so three 9 m safety radars per end watch the far part (8.1 m at most); the scanners keep the side, contact and under-bucket fields. With the scanners alone the forward cap would be 0.31 m/s. The radar is rated for people, not for the test piece, and its use on a moving outdoor machine is to be confirmed with the manufacturer; the check on the hardware is test T4.
- **V-STD-05** (finding): Decided: accepted as a stated residual risk (safety concept, residual risk 11). A scan plane at 70 mm cannot be used on a loader: on gravel and ruts it would see the ground. ISO 3691-4 is written for driverless trucks on flat floors; for autonomous earth-moving machines ISO 17757 relies on a controlled autonomous operating zone. Measures: access control to the operating area (A1). The autonomy side's 3D lidar and camera are the only sensors that could see a person on the ground; they are not safety rated and that case is not tested. The 1:8 testbed's plane is about 0.085 m, so test T4.2 shows the limit rather than hiding it.
- **V-STD-06** (pass): From CAD mass properties; weigh the built machine to confirm.
- **V-STD-07** (open): 4 safety laser scanners 3.20e-07; output stage: brake valve and drive cut-off, two monitored channels 4.29e-08. Output stage MTTFd 100 years per channel at 28000 operations a year; common-cause score claimed 95 (65 needed). Not selected, so no figure: safety radar system (6 sensors, 1 control unit); safety controller. PL d holds if those stay inside the remaining budget. Table K.1 values are from a manufacturer's reproduction and need confirming in the standard. kauha/safety/pl.py.
- **V-PUB-01** (pass): Simulated bucket-pile model against a field result on a larger machine (L180H); it shows the policy is in the published range, not that the model is right.
- **V-PUB-02** (pass): Covers the published sim-to-real force error as a change in digging resistance only.
- **V-PUB-03** (finding): The study gives cycle times only in figures 9 and 10, whose axis ends near 45 s, and reports spread as ratios: productivity differs by a factor of 5 to 8 between operators, 2 to 4 without novices. So the twin's autonomous cycle is at least 1.5 times as long as a human short loading cycle. The causes are the 3 m/s speed cap from the protective field length and a longer travel path in the twin's site. The honest claim is unattended running, not speed. A like-for-like figure needs the pilot site's own operator timing.
- **V-PUB-04** (needs data): The recording's channel list is in its documentation PDF, which could not be read from the build environment. The replay will be written against that list, not guessed.
- **V-DAT-01** (pass): The bus is split: the PLC, sensors and gateway are on a safety segment inside the cabinet; the autonomy computer, remote link and zone source are on an open segment. The guard forwards only four command frames from the open segment, each authenticated with an AES-128 CMAC, a freshness counter and a per-power-cycle session key. Under fuzzing 96 % of genuine commands still got through; the rest are lost and end in a stop by timeout. Limits: a compromised autonomy computer holds its own key and can send any command, which the PLC bounds as before. An attacker with a tool inside the cabinet is on the safety segment, where the CRC alone does not stop forged frames (82 of 200000 random frames accepted at a receiver); that is covered by physical access control, not by the bus. Reference implementation and firmware port, tested against each other; not yet run on the hardware.
- **V-DAT-02** (needs data): Set KAUHA_DATA_ROAD to a folder of candump logs.
- **V-DAT-03** (needs data): Set KAUHA_DATA_SODA to a YOLO-layout copy (images/, labels/).
- **V-DAT-04** (needs data): Set KAUHA_DATA_LIDARDUSTX to the dataset folder (research-use licence).
- **V-DAT-05** (needs data): Set KAUHA_DATA_BOREAS to a folder with two subfolders, snow/ and clear/, each holding Boreas sequences of the same route (boreas-YYYY-MM-DD-HH-MM/lidar/*.bin). Optional KAUHA_BOREAS_SENSOR_HEIGHT in metres (default 2.0).
- **V-DAT-06** (needs data): Set KAUHA_DATA_RADIATE to a folder of RADIATE sequences (each with Navtech_Cartesian/*.png and annotations/annotations.json); fog and snow sequences are the ones of interest. Needs Pillow.
